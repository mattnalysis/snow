HOA Meeting February 19 2023MINUTES

Station Break, Mount Vernon

3pm

Mike Ulrey, Treasurer and acting President (for this meeting), opens the meeting at 3:04pm, as only current officer of the HOA.

He discussed how the last few years have been tough with the pandemic and the dispute about the dam. The HOA has fallen dormant and needs to be revived so that we as a group can effectively respond to the dam issues, maintain community standards, and ultimately decide if we want to continue as an HOA.

Mike then introduced Debbie Banda as a new resident who has volunteered to serve as secretary of the HOA.

For the HOA to be functional again, we need to ensure we are keeping good financial records and have all our member data updated. Members at the meeting have been asked to check and update if necessary, their details on the member lists being circulated. The dam issue is being handled separately by the lawyers. We can discuss this after meeting if needed, but this is not the main topic of this meeting.

HOA has had no income for the last few years. It started with $20k+ from the first few years of its existence, and we still have some left: enough to operate for another 2 years. But we need to decide well before 2024 whether or not to require yearly HOA dues. Mike estimates that around $45 per year per homeowner will be sufficient to meet the current financial needs, absent any special expenses.

Insurance cost is for liability in common areas, even though we don’t have any common areas. The liability insurance does protect the HOA (and its board) against potential lawsuits, so we should keep it.

Mowing cost is for the area on Woodlake Trail near the dam. Do we want to keep doing this? On the one hand, we do not want an unsightly area of overgrown weeds, but on the other hand, we don’t want to give the impression that we are possessive about the lake/dam.

Accounting costs are for submission of taxes and auditing the books. Helps us keep track of check writing, etc. Mike thinks it’s worth it to continue to pay the accountant for these services.

There are some areas where we can trim costs, such as doing the expense charts ourselves (see above). We do not require an extensive 4-page report intended mainly for much larger organizations.

Some members want solar panels approved. Currently prohibited in our deed restrictions. However, Ohio has recently passed a law saying HOAs are not permitted to prevent solar panels. One member said there was a poll pre-covid approving solar panels, but no official vote was taken, as the covid pandemic disrupted normal operations. The issue now is to find out what the law says in terms of what, if any, control an HOA could have over the installation of solar panels. And if so, decide if we want to control or restrict the solar panels in any way. Perhaps we can consult our lawyer members to study the law and make recommendations.

With any deed restrictions, we would need a majority vote of all homeowners. Here is the relevant passage in the HOA covenants:

As a side note, below is a list of three definitions of “Quorum”, depending on the context. These apply to normal business operations in the conduct of, respectively,

(1) A full HOA membership meeting,

(2) A board of trustees meeting only, and

(3) A vote by the full HOA membership to change the “Articles”.

Note that the “Articles” describe how the HOA conducts business, and changing an article is NOT the same as changing a deed restriction (instead see Covenant 22 Modification just above.)

Appendix 1: Ohio State General Assembly Senate Bill 61 (in effect Sep 2022)

Sec. 5311.192. (A) Unless specifically prohibited in the declaration, any owner of a solar appropriate unit may install a solar energy collection device on the roof of that unit if either of the following conditions apply: (1) The unit, as defined by the declaration, includes the roof, for which the cost to insure, maintain, repair, and replace is not a common expense and is instead the owner's responsibility. (2) The declaration specifically allows for and regulates the types and installation of solar energy collection devices in the common or limited common elements and establishes responsibility for the cost to insure, maintain, repair, and replace such devices. (B) Notwithstanding division (A) of this section, a unit owners association may establish reasonable restrictions concerning the size, place, and manner of placement of solar energy collection devices. (C) As used in this section: (1) "Solar appropriate unit" means a condominium unit that does not have any other Sub. S. B. No. 61 134th G.A. 17 condominium units directly above or below it. (2) "Solar energy collection device" means any device manufactured and sold for the sole purpose of facilitating the collection and beneficial use of solar energy, including passive heating panels or building components and solar photovoltaic apparatus.
